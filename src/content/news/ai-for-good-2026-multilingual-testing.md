ITU has published its write-up of the AI for Good Global Summit 2026, held in Geneva from 7 to 10 July: [From principles to practice: Standards for interoperable and inclusive AI](https://s41721.pcdn.co/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/AIStandardsReport2026.pdf), the AI Standards Report 2026. Multilingual testing comes up across several of its workshops. These are the points that matter most for anyone evaluating AI outside English.

## Tests built elsewhere miss local harms

The workshop on testing and verification of frontier AI systems (chapter 33) puts it plainly:

> Evaluation datasets and safety tests created outside a target context may miss local linguistic, cultural and social realities.

It adds that "test sentences that are grammatically correct may still be socially unrealistic in local languages", and that bias can be specific to a place: age bias, for example, shows up differently from region to region. Translating an English test set is not enough.

The panel called for "co-created evaluation frameworks, feedback mechanisms, local participation and recurring updates".

## The gaps, in the report's own table

Table 8 lists the gaps the workshop found. Two are about where testing happens:

- **Context and cultural gaps.** Models may behave differently across languages, cultures and social contexts. The response: co-create evaluation datasets and context-specific test scenarios with local stakeholders.
- **Capacity asymmetry.** Many countries depend on testing performed elsewhere. The response: build public evaluation infrastructure and regional testing capacity, with common toolkits, reporting templates and capacity-building curricula.

## Multilingual test data

The foundation model evaluation session (chapter 19) closed with a call to "accelerate the construction of more comprehensive, diverse, and multilingual high-quality testing datasets that adequately reflect application scenarios across different cultural and linguistic contexts", and to ensure that "the Global South and developing economies benefit equitably".

## Our talk

Section 19.4, on standardization pathways for foundation models, records Adrianna Tan's contribution:

> Adrianna Tan, Founder of Future Ethics Lab, discussed red teaming, benchmarks and other tools for downstream AI assurance, highlighting multilingual and cross-cultural gaps in current testing, the value of broader public participation, and the importance of communicating evaluation in plain language.

The full report is free to read. Section 19.4 is on page 125.
