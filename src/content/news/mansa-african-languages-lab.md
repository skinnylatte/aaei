African Languages Lab has [launched Mansa](https://multilingual.com/african-languages-lab-launches-mansa-multilingual-multimodal-ai-africa/), a multilingual, multimodal AI system built for African languages, accents and contexts. It is available on the web and mobile, with an API for developers and businesses.

**The gap it targets.** According to the launch announcement, African languages make up less than 1% of the data used to train modern AI. In Common Crawl, English is over 40%, while the 24 African languages it tracks, including Swahili, Hausa, Yoruba and Zulu, add up to less than 0.05%.

**How it was built.** "Much of Africa's knowledge was never written down. It is spoken," said founder Sheriff Issaka. The Lab spent a decade collecting data language by language and community by community, through open-licensed sources, community partnerships, and All Voices, its platform where native speakers contribute and validate data. The collection spans more than 70 languages. Thirty are in production in Mansa, built on over 100 billion curated tokens and more than 19,000 hours of expert-validated speech.

**What it covers.** More than 30 African languages, including Twi, Hausa, Ewe, Swahili, Igbo, Amharic and Zulu, alongside English, French, Spanish and Chinese, with regional dialects and code-switching.

**What is live.** Chat, an agent that browses and runs multi-step workflows, translation, speech-to-text tuned for regional accents, text-to-speech, and real-time interpretation between two speakers.

## Why we are watching it

Mansa is a model built from data its speakers collected and checked, rather than English data translated outward. That is the approach the evaluation community keeps arguing for. It also raises the next question: as systems like this reach banks, telcos and health services, they need testing in the same languages, accents and code-switched speech they serve, by people who speak them.
